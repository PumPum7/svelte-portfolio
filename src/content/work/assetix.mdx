## Product

Assetix was built during StartHack 2026 as a financial-literacy game. Stocks, bonds, currencies, indices, crypto, and real assets become collectible **FinMons** with their own stats and abilities. Players learn the underlying concepts, build a team, and test those choices through battles and portfolio simulations.

## Product system

The product combines a guided learning journey, collectible progression, team selection, market-event battles, and a sandbox for comparing portfolio allocations against historical data. The game layer gives abstract asset classes distinct behaviour instead of presenting financial education as another list of definitions.

The stack spans a Next.js interface, a FastAPI service, PostgreSQL persistence, and a Docker-based development and deployment setup.

## The Rust battle engine

The battle engine is the project's most distinct technical piece. It is written in Rust, compiled to WebAssembly, and runs the turn-based simulation in the browser.

The backend supplies validated teams, battle mode, and a deterministic seed. The engine then owns round resolution: asset-class abilities, team swaps, cooldowns, shields, market events, opponent decisions, and the win condition. Sprint, standard, and marathon modes change team size and battle length without moving the game loop back into the interface.

This boundary kept the simulation separate from React state and gave the battle rules one compact runtime. The server remains responsible for user collections, matchmaking inputs, progression, rewards, and recording the final result.

## Constraints

- A hackathon schedule required one coherent product path rather than separate polished demos.
- Financial concepts had to remain recognizable after being translated into game mechanics.
- Client-side battle state needed repeatable inputs and a clear handoff back to server-side progression.
- A playful pixel-art interface still had to support data-heavy portfolio tools.
- Frontend, API, database, WebAssembly build, and deployment had to work as one stack.

## Outcome

The result is a working full-stack prototype with authentication, collection and progression systems, educational journeys, portfolio simulation, and browser-based FinMon battles. The public repository includes the application, Rust engine, data, sprites, Docker setup, and deployment workflow.

## Reflection

Assetix worked because the game mechanics were tied to the subject: bonds heal through coupon payments, equities compound, indices reduce variance, and market events change the value of each choice. The Rust engine made those rules explicit while the rest of the product handled learning, identity, and progression.

With more time, the next step would be narrowing the product around its strongest learning loop and testing whether players understand the financial trade-offs after the battle, not only whether they win it.
